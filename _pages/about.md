## About Me

I am a climate student interested in understanding how anthropogenic warming reorganizes the coupled climate system, especially the tropical ocean-atmosphere interaction. My research combines observations, theory, and climate model simulations to investigate the physical mechanisms linking ocean circulation, sea surface temperature (SST) patterns, atmospheric circulation, and cloud feedbacks.

I recently completed my M.S. in Atmospheric Sciences at National Taiwan University under the supervision of Prof. Yen-Ting Hwang. During my graduate studies, I also worked as a Summer Visiting Student in Prof. Tiffany A. Shaw's research group at the University of Chicago. My research spans tropical climate dynamics, ocean–atmosphere interaction, and climate model evaluation, with an emphasis on understanding forced climate responses and improving confidence in future climate projections.

## Current Research

### Pacific Subtropical Cells in a Warming Climate
*Master's thesis · Manuscript in preparation*

My master's research investigates how anthropogenic warming alters the dynamics of Pacific Subtropical Cells (STCs). Using CESM2 Large Ensemble simulations together with theoretical diagnostics, I examine how density-driven ocean circulation gradually overtakes traditional wind-driven mechanisms under future climate change.


### Tropical Cloud Responses to SST Warming Patterns
*Manuscript in preparation*

This work investigates the physical mechanisms linking recent tropical cloud contraction with SST warming patterns and large-scale atmospheric circulation using observations, reanalysis datasets, CMIP6 simulations, and Pacific Pacemaker experiments.
