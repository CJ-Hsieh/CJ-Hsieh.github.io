## About Me

I am a climate scientist interested in understanding how anthropogenic warming reorganizes the coupled tropical ocean–atmosphere system. My research combines observations, theory, and climate model simulations to investigate the physical mechanisms linking ocean circulation, sea surface temperature (SST) patterns, atmospheric circulation, and cloud feedbacks.

I recently completed my M.S. in Atmospheric Sciences at National Taiwan University under the supervision of Prof. Yen-Ting Hwang. During my graduate study, I also worked as a Summer Visiting Student in Prof. Tiffany A. Shaw's research group at the University of Chicago. My research spans tropical climate dynamics, ocean–atmosphere interaction, and climate model evaluation, with an emphasis on understanding forced climate responses and improving confidence in future climate projections.

## Current Research

### Pacific Subtropical Cell Changes under Anthropogenic Warming

My master's research investigates how anthropogenic warming alters the dynamics of Pacific Subtropical Cells (STCs). Using CESM2 Large Ensemble simulations together with theoretical diagnostics, I examine how density-driven ocean circulation gradually overtakes traditional wind-driven mechanisms under future climate change.

### Tropical Cloud Contraction and SST Warming Patterns

My current work investigates the physical mechanisms linking recent tropical cloud contraction with SST warming patterns and large-scale atmospheric circulation using observations, reanalysis datasets, CMIP6 simulations, and PacPACE experiments.

## Research Vision

A central question motivating my research is:

> **How does anthropogenic warming reorganize tropical ocean–atmosphere interactions, and how do these changes shape future climate patterns?**

Rather than studying individual components of the climate system in isolation, I aim to understand how ocean circulation, SST patterns, clouds, and atmospheric dynamics interact to produce the observed and projected responses of the tropical climate system.

I hope this website provides an overview of my research, publications, and presentations.
